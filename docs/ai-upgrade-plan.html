Where Blackbox still loses

  • Distribution and trust. No users, reviews or college partnerships yet; every competitor above has them.
  • Résumé upload. Final Round, Huru and Sparklane build interviews from a résumé. We tailor to a pasted job description and the profile, not yet a résumé file.
  • Coding round. DevInterview.AI and Pramp have a runnable code editor; we have none.
  • Human experts. interviewing.io sells real engineers; we have no human review path.
  • Languages and mobile. Huru and Verve support many languages and a phone app; we are English-only and web-only.

Novelty and USPs

What no single competitor above combines today.

The whole placement loop in one placeMock interviews, AI group discussions with real floor control, a 500-question bank and project defence, scored on one readiness record. Interview-only tools skip the GD round; GD tools have thin interviews.
Private by constructionWhisper transcription and eye-contact analysis run in the browser; recordings and video never leave the device. Text that does leave is PII-masked, including Aadhaar and PAN, which matters under India's DPDP Act.
Works without AI or internet to the modelA curated bank, rule-based scoring and an offline planner mean a student with no paid key still gets the full product. Competitors stop working without their backend model.
Feedback you can checkCoaching and study pointers cite retrieved passages, and the planner shows the tool calls behind its plan. Most tools give unexplained scores.
An interviewer that feels humanEmotion-directed voice, follow-ups on your own projects, hints when you're stuck, an editable transcript. Most voice mocks read questions in one flat tone.
Practice, never cheatingNo live "copilot" that feeds answers during real interviews. Reviewers flag that as the main ethics risk of Final Round, Verve and LockedIn; it is our trust advantage with colleges.

RAG

Grounding the interviewer and coach in content we control.

Where it is used

FeatureQueryWhat retrieval changes
Coach chatMessage + open questionAnswers cite [1], [2] with sources; offline answers are built from retrieved notes instead of a canned message.
Interviewer coachingThe question the candidate is stuck onHints come from the study notes and model answers, not model memory.
Per-answer feedbackThe questionIdeal answer and "what to study" are grounded; offline uses the closest bank answer.
Job-description tailoringPasted job descriptionOffline, the technical questions are the bank questions closest to the job; online, the JD shapes the prompt.
Prep PlannerWeak topicsThe agent's search_knowledge tool picks what to study.

Design

  • Corpus (lib/rag/sync.ts): question bank, curated notes (lib/rag/knowledge.ts), company profiles, GD topics. Content-hashed, so re-sync only touches changed chunks. Runs on every seed and via npm run rag:ingest.
  • Keyword: generated tsvector column with a GIN index, title weighted above body, re-ranked by query-term coverage.
  • Vector: gemini-embedding-001 at 768 dimensions, normalised, cached in memory (about 6 MB). Queries are PII-masked before embedding.
  • Fusion: reciprocal rank fusion (k = 60) plus small boosts for the candidate's track and the question's category.

Next

  • Move vectors to pgvector with an HNSW index once the corpus passes ~20k chunks.
  • Résumé upload as a per-user, private collection (masked, deletable), retrieved for project follow-ups.
  • A cross-encoder re-ranker if MRR stays below 0.8 with embeddings on.

Online and offline

The question asked was whether to use Gemini online and the stored questions offline. Yes, and it is built that way.

Gemini makes the interviewer adaptive, grounds feedback, voices lines expressively and lets the planner reason. The curated bank keeps the product useful with no key, no budget or a failed connection, and it is the source RAG grounds Gemini in. A circuit breaker switches modes automatically after three failures and retries after a minute; the top bar tells the candidate which mode they are in.

One limit to state plainly: "offline" means offline from the AI provider. The app still needs its own server. A fully offline phone experience would need a PWA with a cached question subset, which is on the roadmap.

Risks

  • Model and key cost. Mitigated by offline mode, rate limits on AI routes and per-feature audit counts; set a monthly budget alert in Google AI Studio.
  • Small eval sets. Today's gates prove the pipeline, not production quality. Phase 1 grows them from real, masked sessions.
  • Heuristic PII detection misses names it has never seen in free text. Mitigation: the candidate's own name is always masked; add an NER pass if audits show leaks.
  • Per-instance rate limiter. Move to Redis before running more than one server instance.
